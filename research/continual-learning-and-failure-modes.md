# Continual Learning for Trading Models & Failure Modes of Autonomous Trading Systems

**Provenance:** Researched by a Sonnet subagent, 2026-08-01, via WebSearch/WebFetch/Exa. Part of a 5-agent parallel sweep on autonomous strategy-discovery systems for a $100k-$1M crypto trading project. Report-only, no files modified by the subagent.

## TOPIC A — Online/Continual Learning

### A.1 Catastrophic forgetting

Origin: McCloskey, M. & Cohen, N.J. (1989), "Catastrophic Interference in Connectionist Networks: The Sequential Learning Problem," Psychology of Learning and Motivation, Vol. 24 — trained a net sequentially on two arithmetic-fact tasks; performance on the first collapsed almost immediately once the second task's gradient updates began, far more severe than human forgetting. Mechanism: a neural net's knowledge is stored as distributed, overlapping weights, not localized per-task; unconstrained gradient steps that reduce loss on new data overwrite the weight configuration encoding old behavior.

Modern survey: Parisi, Kemker, Part, Kanan, Wermter (2019), "Continual Lifelong Learning with Neural Networks: A Review," Neural Networks, 113:54–71. Companion classic: French, R.M. (1999), "Catastrophic Forgetting in Connectionist Networks," Trends in Cognitive Sciences.

Naive online fine-tuning on recent market data risks silently destroying a model's ability to handle a regime it hasn't seen in months — undetected until that regime recurs, likely mid-trade with capital at risk.

### A.2 Mitigations — honest domain-gap assessment

- **EWC**: Kirkpatrick et al. (2017), "Overcoming Catastrophic Forgetting in Neural Networks," PNAS. Quadratic penalty (weighted by Fisher information) discouraging changes to weights important for prior tasks.
- Also: experience replay/rehearsal, synaptic intelligence (Zenke et al. 2017), gradient episodic memory (Lopez-Paz & Ranzato 2017), progressive networks, knowledge distillation.

**Domain-gap findings (most important part of this section)**:
1. EWC and peers were validated almost entirely on vision classification (split-MNIST, CIFAR) and RL game benchmarks with clean task boundaries — trading is continuous, non-stationary, with fuzzy regime boundaries.
2. Ehret et al. (2021), "Continual Learning in Recurrent Neural Networks" (published, per grewelab.org citation): weight-importance methods like EWC specifically struggle on RNNs — the natural architecture family for sequential market data — because high working-memory requirements saturate Fisher-based importance estimates, making the network rigid rather than appropriately plastic. Direct published warning that EWC doesn't transfer cleanly from feedforward vision nets to sequence models.
3. Philps, A. et al. (2019), "Making Good on LSTMs' Unfulfilled Promise" (Warwick Business School/Gillmore Centre working paper): tested CL on a real financial time-series task (EM equity selection); standard LSTMs underperformed even a simple CL-augmented feedforward sliding-window model; a finance-specific memory method ("CLA," dynamic time warping-based recall) beat generic CL approaches. Authors' own framing: "It is unclear how effective these [generic CL] approaches would be in dealing with long-term continual learning of noisy, non-stationary time-series, commonly found in finance."
4. Trading-specific CL papers reporting gains do exist: Katsikas, Passalis & Tefas (ICPR 2024), "Plasticity Driven Knowledge Transfer for Continual DRL in Financial Trading" (experience replay + teacher-ensemble transfer, crypto DRL trading, beats greedily-fine-tuned baselines); Pan et al. (2026 preprint, arXiv:2606.00143), "Regime-Adaptive Continual Learning for Portfolio Management" (ReCAP) — 164.89% cumulative return/1.14 Sharpe vs 124.24%/0.92 for plain rolling-window retraining, 5 real datasets, 17 years. Genuine but recent (2024-2026), narrow academic benchmarks, none report live/paper-trading validation beyond backtest.
5. Philps et al. (2018), "Continual Learning Augmented Investment Decisions" (arXiv:1812.02340) — early attempt, explicit the field is thin.

**Bottom line**: catastrophic forgetting is real and mechanistically well-understood; EWC/replay have a genuine but young, narrow (mostly 2019-2026, single-paper) finance literature, mostly backtested not live-validated. At least one direct study (Ehret et al. 2021) shows EWC has known weaknesses on RNN/sequence architectures trading naturally uses. Treat vendor/paper claims that CL "solves" regime drift for trading with real skepticism — domain transfer not proven at production scale.

### A.3 What practitioners actually use

Fancy CL ML is NOT dominant in practice. Three simpler tools dominate:

1. **Walk-forward optimization/rolling-window retraining** — train on a rolling window, test on next OOS slice, roll forward, repeat. Standard default per QuantConnect docs, QuantInsti, IBKR Quant News, Wikipedia. More rigorous variant: CPCV (López de Prado) described by practitioners as the expensive thorough check run once on the survivor, not for iterative development (combinatorial compute cost).
2. **Regime detection + model switching**, most commonly via **Hidden Markov Models**. Practitioner sources (QuantStart, Bocconi BSIC, QuestDB glossary): classify market into latent states, switch strategy sleeves/cut leverage/widen-tighten stops based on detected regime, rather than making one model adapt continuously. Sidesteps catastrophic forgetting entirely by maintaining separate regime-conditioned strategies rather than overwriting one model's knowledge.
3. **Ensembles/strategy portfolios with performance-based reweighting** — allocate across multiple uncorrelated strategies, dynamically down-weight/pause unhealthy ones, rather than retraining any single model online.

Directly relevant recent pattern: **Zhang et al., "Train Often, Deploy Selectively: Forward-Gated Model Replacement in Crypto Markets"** (2026 preprint, arXiv:2607.28577) — "Shadow Before Swap" (SBS): retrain a challenger on schedule but only promote it over the live incumbent after a shadow trial on the next week's delayed labels shows a real, pre-registered advantage. Reported: 78.4% fewer deployed-state changes with equal/better forecast quality vs. blind promote-on-retrain, evaluated on crypto forecasting. Narrowly-scoped, non-exotic, directly applicable to a retrain-vs-deploy gate independent of whether the underlying model uses CL or plain retraining.

**Which dominates**: walk-forward retraining is unambiguously the workhorse (taught everywhere, built into retail platforms, no exotic infra). HMM regime detection is second most common, used to decide when/whether to retrain rather than to prevent forgetting per se. CL ML techniques (EWC, replay architectures) appear almost exclusively in academic papers (2019-2026), not standard tooling on any retail/small-fund practitioner platform found.

### A.4 Retrain vs. retire criteria

"Alpha decay" concept sources: Bailey & López de Prado's overfitting work; Exegy industry blog; Maven Securities; tradingengineeringlab.com.

Distilled framework (converging across sources, but note several specific sources here are practitioner/hobbyist blogs of unverified authorship — concepts are standard/consistent with established literature, specific numeric thresholds are illustrative not authoritative):

| Signal pattern | Likely cause | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Sharpe/win-rate declines gradually, costs/slippage rise in step | Alpha crowding (others found and are arbitraging the signal) | Retire or shrink capacity — retraining doesn't fix competition |
| Sharpe declines gradually, costs flat, other stats shift with market cycle | Regime drift (logic still holds, needs recalibration) | Retrain/walk-forward roll |
| Sharp step-down tied to a venue/fee/tick-size/rule change date | Microstructure change | Usually retire — the exploited substrate changed |
| Live diverges from a fixed-size shadow version at larger size, shadow book still healthy | Capacity saturation from own size, not decay | Reduce allocation, not retrain |
| Repeated retraining "fixes" performance short-term but each version underperforms sooner ("chasing") | Structural break disguised as drift | Retire — further retraining fits noise |

Core heuristic: a statistical/soft signal (Sharpe dip, drawdown within historical Monte Carlo bounds) should trigger investigation, not action; two independent signals together, or one structural signal (provable external cause), should trigger retirement/size reduction. Pre-committing thresholds before a drawdown starts (not deciding in the moment) defends against both over-hasty killing of a healthy strategy in a normal drawdown and over-long holding of a dead one from sunk-cost bias.

## TOPIC B — Failure Modes

### B.1 Knight Capital, Aug 1 2012 — $440-460M in 45 minutes

Confirmed mechanism (SEC Release No. 70694; Doug Seven's widely-cited "Knightmare: A DevOps Cautionary Tale"): legacy "Power Peg" code, unused ~8 years, controlled by a flag. In 2012 Knight repurposed the *same flag* to activate a new feature (RLP) instead of creating a new flag, and never deleted the dead Power Peg code. During manual deployment (no automated verified rollout), a technician failed to copy new code to 1 of 8 production servers, no second-engineer review. On market open, 7 servers ran RLP correctly; the 8th's flag reactivated dormant Power Peg, which lacked modern order-fulfillment tracking and entered a runaway buy/sell loop. ~4 million executions across 154 stocks, ~397 million shares, in ~45 minutes before diagnosis/stop. SEC action (Oct 2013) cited code review/change management failures, no algorithm inventory, insufficient migration testing, no effective real-time monitoring/kill-switch (general characterization well-corroborated; exact statutory citation not independently verified — PDF extraction was garbled).

**Lesson for autonomous systems**: a deployment/config-safety failure, not a strategy-discovery failure, but directly relevant: dead code paths and flag reuse are a live-fire risk multiplier in any system that reactivates old logic under new conditions. For a strategy-discovery system that promotes/demotes strategies and reuses infrastructure, the equivalent risk is a retired strategy's execution path/parameter set/config flag being silently reactivated by a promotion/retirement state-machine bug, without the safety checks the current strategy generation has. No single point of manual, unverified deployment should be able to put live capital at risk — automated identical verified rollout across every execution node, plus a fast tested kill switch, are non-negotiable.

### B.2 May 6 2010 Flash Crash

Source: SEC/CFTC joint staff report, "Findings Regarding the Market Events of May 6, 2010" (sec.gov/files/marketevents-report.pdf, released Sept 30 2010). Document existence confirmed at official URL; full-text extraction failed in this session, so mechanism description relies on well-corroborated secondary sources (Lexology, CFTC staff report page, law-review commentary), not a fresh primary-text read.

Widely reported mechanism: amid an already turbulent day, a large fundamental trader executed a very large E-Mini S&P 500 sell program (~75,000 contracts) via an execution algorithm sizing participation by *volume* without regard to price/time. HFT firms' algorithms absorbed and rapidly resold contracts to each other ("hot potato"), spiking volume, which caused the parent algorithm to execute faster, accelerating the decline it was itself causing. Many HFT/market-making algorithms hit risk limits and withdrew liquidity entirely rather than widen quotes, causing a liquidity vacuum. Result: ~9% intraday drop and recovery within minutes, some stocks traded to a penny.

**Lesson**: canonical demonstration that a fleet of independently "correct" automated systems, each rational under local logic, can produce systemic catastrophe with no single buggy component. For a system promoting new strategies into production: the risk isn't just "does this strategy work standalone" but what happens when its behavior interacts with the rest of the strategy fleet and market-wide liquidity conditions under stress — a newly-promoted strategy needs its own hard circuit breakers (max participation rate, price-band sanity checks, automatic pause on abnormal volatility).

### B.3 ML-driven/"self-improving" trading system failures — documented cases

**Strongest, multi-source corroborated, real-money, publicly auditable:**

- **Alpha Arena / Nof1.ai (Oct-Dec 2025)**: real-money benchmark, six frontier LLMs trading crypto perpetuals autonomously on Hyperliquid, each given $10,000, fully public on-chain trades, no human intervention, same prompt/harness. Corroborated: Nof1's own blog, South China Morning Post, news.Bitcoin.com. Season 1 (Oct 18-Nov 3 2025): Qwen3-Max +22.32%, DeepSeek V3.1 +4.89%; **Claude Sonnet 4.5 -30.81%, Grok 4 -45.3%, Gemini 2.5 Pro -56.71%, GPT-5 -62.66%.** Nof1's postmortem attributes losses largely to over-trading (Gemini: 238 trades, ~13%+ of capital lost to fees), rigid directional bias with no hedging (Grok 4: 100% long throughout, no stop-loss discipline), decision paralysis under conflicting signals (GPT-5). Nof1 itself cautions results "may be the result of luck" given the short 17-day window and notes high sensitivity to small prompt changes. Most directly relevant documented case: real capital, fully autonomous LLM-driven trading, crypto perpetuals, transparently audited.
- **Alameda Research fat-finger flash crash, Oct 21 2021**: manual (not automated) trading interface, decimal-point mis-place selling a BTC block, Binance.US BTC briefly crashed ~$65,760 to ~$8,200. Corroborated via SBF criminal trial testimony (Business Insider) and a first-hand Alameda engineer account (Aditya Baradwaj's blog, corroborated by IBTimes). Losses "tens of millions." Culturally notable: per the engineer, Alameda's philosophy was explicitly "the utility we gained by moving fast outweighed the occasional costs we paid due to poor risk checks" — a firm knowingly underinvesting in exactly the sanity-check/circuit-breaker infrastructure this report recommends, until forced to retrofit after a large loss.
- **FTX/Alameda's $8B accounting-ledger bug**: not a trading-model failure, but directly relevant — a bug in customer-deposit automation code caused Alameda's liabilities on FTX's internal ledger to be understated, growing from ~$500M to $8B over ~6 months before being fixed (per sworn trial testimony, Business Insider). Illustrates how a purely software bug in an automated trading/exchange system can compound silently for months before detection.
- **Lobstar Wilde (Feb 2026)**: autonomous AI trading/token-management agent built on the "OpenClaw" framework by an individual OpenAI employee, misread a social-media request for "4 SOL," transferred ~52.4 million tokens (~$250K-$441K) to a stranger, reportedly a decimal/units confusion plus loss of conversational state after a crash. Corroborated: Finance Magnates, Yahoo Finance, a crypto Medium retrospective, all citing PANews/The Block. Concrete illustration of state-management/unit-handling bugs, directly relevant to "silent state corruption" risk in an always-on autonomous system.

**Moderate corroboration — single detailed source, plausible/specific, not independently cross-verified:**

- **"We Built a 65-Feature ML Pipeline and It Lost 89%"** (Uptrail blog, 2026): commodity-futures ML pipeline (fractional differentiation, triple-barrier labeling, purged CV, meta-labeling — following López de Prado's AFML), 55% OOS directional accuracy, lost 89% live due to a structural short bias learned from a 2014-2023 range-bound training period deployed into a 2024-2026 bull run. Internally consistent numbers; failure mechanism (accuracy ≠ profitability; label-distribution shift between train and deploy) is well-known and credible. Could not independently verify the underlying account/results — treat as plausible detailed practitioner account, not audited case.
- **"The Red Queen's Trap: Limits of Deep Evolution in High-Frequency Trading"** (Y. Chen, arXiv:2512.15732, Dec 2025 preprint, NOT confirmed peer-reviewed): postmortem of "Galaxy Empire," a DRL + genetic-evolution hybrid, 500 autonomous agents, live HFT crypto — reports validation APY >300% vs. live capital decay >70%. Attributes gap to overfitting of aleatoric uncertainty in low-entropy time series, survivor bias inherent to evolutionary selection under high variance, microstructure friction. Exactly the "self-improving discovery system overfitting in production" pattern asked about, but unconfirmed peer review/replication — credible-sounding, unverified single-author account.
- **Polymarket-autopsy** (public GitHub/README, solo builder, Apr-Jun 2026): 45-day, $1,500-capital crypto/prediction-market bot project — three classes of measurement bugs inflated apparent paper-trading PnL ~135× (reported paper PnL $157,115 vs corrected clean paper PnL $1,162); live deployment lost money (-$96 on $300 real capital) despite paper trading looking profitable. Spearman rank correlation between paper EV and live EV across 6 deployed architectures was **-0.43** — paper-trading results were *inversely* predictive of live performance; the bots ranked best in paper-trading performed worst live. Small personal project, but an unusually honest, well-documented, quantified example of exactly the silent-degradation pattern.

**Overall assessment**: institutional-grade peer-reviewed postmortems of specific named self-improving/autonomous trading systems failing in production are scarce (funds rarely publish postmortems; commercial incentive is the opposite). What exists: one high-quality transparent real-money public benchmark (Alpha Arena, 4 of 6 frontier LLMs lost 30-63% autonomously trading crypto with no oversight), several corroborated crypto-industry incident reports of autonomous agents making costly state/unit-handling errors, and a thin recent (2025-2026) layer of arXiv preprints/detailed practitioner blog postmortems describing the classic overfitting-in-production pattern, none with the institutional weight of an SEC enforcement action.

### B.4 Silent degradation — look-ahead bias, survivorship bias, promotion-gate failures

- **Survivorship bias in crypto**: an academic paper "Survivorship and Delisting Bias in Cryptocurrency Markets" (University of St. Gallen, Alexandria research repository) exists (full text fetch failed, 405 error — headline stats unverified, but existence as peer-reviewed-adjacent treatment is confirmed). Secondary sources (CoinAPI, Concretum Group): of ~24,000 tokens ever listed on CoinMarketCap since 2013, >14,000 (>58%) are now dead. Cited example: a "buy top-20 altcoins by market cap" backtest showed +2,800% return with survivorship bias vs +680% without — >4x inflation from this single bias. A claim that "Coinbase Institutional Research found survivorship bias inflates backtested returns by 17-22% annually" appears only in secondary blog summaries, not traced to a primary Coinbase source — **UNVERIFIED figure**, though the general phenomenon is well-established.
- **Look-ahead/leakage bias — rigorous recent treatment**: Zhang, Li, Peng, Chen (2026 preprint, arXiv:2605.23959), "When Alpha Disappears: A One-Switch Benchmark for Decision-Time Leakage in Financial Backtests" (Univ. of Tokyo/HKUST Guangzhou). Isolates five leakage mechanisms individually (future-window-centered rolling features; full-sample-fit normalization; symmetric correlation graphs using future returns; same-day-close execution assumptions; using full-daily-bar info while assuming same-day-open fills); finds two — future-window-centered features, and assuming intraday execution at prices only knowable after the fact — dominate, inflating Sharpe ratios by 20+ points for Ridge/tree/graph models, while other leakage types are weak. Core finding directly relevant to a promotion gate: **"chronological order alone does not define what information a model was allowed to use"** — a backtest can be scrupulously time-ordered/walk-forward and still leak through feature engineering or normalization choices touching full-sample statistics.
- A described case (dibi8.com, provenance unverified) of a config schema update silently dropping unknown fields via Python's `dataclass.from_dict()`, defaulting leverage to 10x instead of intended 2x, producing a backtest signature (Train PF 2.08 → OOS PF 0.94) that looked exactly like textbook overfitting when the real cause was a silently-swallowed schema mismatch — a standard, credible software-engineering failure mode regardless of whether this specific post is real, and a direct warning against config-loading libraries without strict validation.

### B.5 Irreproducibility

- **Bailey & López de Prado (2014), "The Deflated Sharpe Ratio..."** (see multiple-comparisons file for full citation): when a strategy is selected as best out of N backtested variants, its reported Sharpe is inflated by the selection process itself, and more variants tried means more deflation needed to estimate true significance. Directly relevant: any automated strategy-discovery system searching over many variants makes this bias apply, and a naive "promote the backtest winner" policy guarantees overstated expectations even with zero coding bugs.
- A secondary-summary finding (original study not independently verified/traced to named authors — **UNVERIFIED**): a study of 888 algorithmic strategies found backtested Sharpe ratios were poor predictors of real-world performance (R² < 0.025), and ~44% of published strategies failed to replicate backtested success on new data. Directionally consistent with the broader overfitting literature but the specific statistic is unverified.
- Practitioner synthesis on the execution-friction gap (distinct from statistical overfitting): backtests commonly assume frictionless fills at mid-price or prices not truly achievable given historical volume at that timestamp, understating slippage/market impact, and stop-losses become aggressive market orders (not guaranteed prices) during real stress events. A strategy can be statistically sound and still fail live purely on execution-cost modeling error — needs separate non-linear volume-dependent impact modeling and realistic fee/spread assumptions.
- The polymarket-autopsy case (B.3) is also directly relevant here: paper-trading results actively anti-correlated (Spearman ρ=-0.43) with subsequent live results, due to unnoticed measurement bugs in the paper-trading harness itself.
