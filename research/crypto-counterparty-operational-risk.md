## 1. Exchange downtime during high volatility

**May 19, 2021 flash crash (BTC -30% intraday):** Binance and Coinbase both suffered documented outages simultaneously with the crash. Binance halted retail trading and — per an academic study using 73M transaction records — stopped providing transaction-level API data for ~40 minutes while its liquidation engine kept running in the background; backfilled data later failed a Benford's Law authenticity check, and litigation followed alleging the outage benefited Binance's insurance fund. VERIFIED (Bloomberg, May 19 2021; Baumgartner & Guettler, IWH Halle discussion paper 2022/25, SSRN 4260876). Coinbase reported "intermittent downtime," some users unable to log in, see balances, or trade. VERIFIED (Bloomberg, NY Post, May 19 2021). Kraken also reported client disruptions the same day. VERIFIED (Bloomberg).

**January 6–7, 2021:** Coinbase's API and mobile apps went down for hours during a BTC run to $40k, caused by MongoDB cluster overload and a cascading "retry storm." Coinbase's own postmortem states trading was functional but account access/API was not, for a multi-hour window each day. VERIFIED (Coinbase Blog postmortem, Jan 8 2021).

**May 12–13, 2022 (Terra/UST collapse, BTC below $30k):** Coinbase had a major outage — users unable to trade or withdraw for roughly an hour amid the crash. VERIFIED (The Verge, DataCenterDynamics, May 12–13 2022).

**November 8, 2022 (FTX collapse, BTC breaks below $18k):** Kraken and Coinbase both reported "connectivity issues" within an hour of each other — users unable to log in or trade — fueling bank-run rumors. VERIFIED (Decrypt, CryptoSlate, Nov 8 2022).

**February 19, 2020 and March 12, 2020:** Binance halted all trading/deposits/withdrawals twice within weeks for "unscheduled maintenance" during volatile periods; OKEx also did unscheduled maintenance mid-volatility the same week. VERIFIED (CryptoPotato, Cointelegraph, 2020).

**August 5, 2024 (yen carry-trade unwind):** BTC/ETH fell ~18–25%, Nikkei had its worst day since 1987, over $1B in crypto liquidated in 24h across Binance, HTX, Bybit, BitMEX, OKX. No exchange-specific outage claim found for this date (unlike Oct 2025) — treat as a high-volatility event without a confirmed matching-engine failure. UNVERIFIED for outage specifically (price action VERIFIED via CoinDesk, BIS Bulletin No. 90).

**October 10–11, 2025 ("10/10 crash," largest liquidation event in crypto history, $19B+ liquidated, 1.6M accounts):** This is the most severe and best-documented recent case. Binance experienced roughly an hour (20:50–22:00 UTC) of degraded service — frozen charts, hung orders, some users locked out entirely, plus API failures and deposit delays — while its Unified Account margin system used internal spot prices (not external oracles) to value collateral (USDe, WBETH, BNSOL), causing those assets to depeg *only on Binance* (USDe fell to $0.65 vs. $1.00 elsewhere) and triggering cascading forced liquidations. Binance later paid $283M in user compensation. dYdX was offline ~8 hours; Lighter had a 4.5-hour outage. Hyperliquid (a DEX) reported 100% uptime and processed nearly half of global liquidations on-chain. VERIFIED (CoinDesk Oct 11 2025; Forbes Oct 13 2025; CoinGecko explainer; Binance's own public incident report referenced in coverage). The OKX CEO publicly disputed Binance's account of root cause — that dispute is itself evidence the "official" outage narrative from an exchange should not be taken at face value.

**Pattern:** every major volatility spike since 2020 has a matching exchange degradation event on at least one top-5 venue. This is not tail risk — it is close to a base rate.

## 2. API rate limits and ban policy (from official docs)

- **Binance:** Weight-based system (not flat requests/min); default IP limit shared across `/api` endpoints is 6,000 weight/minute (varies by doc version, historically cited as 1,200/min); heavier endpoints cost more weight. HTTP 429 = rate-limited (must back off); HTTP 418 = IP auto-banned for continuing after 429s. Bans scale 2 minutes → 3 days for repeat offenses. Limits are IP-based, not API-key-based — meaning multiple bots behind one IP/NAT share the same budget. VERIFIED (github.com/binance/binance-spot-api-docs, developers.binance.com).
- **Bybit:** 600 requests/5 sec per IP (hard IP throttle, 403 + 10-minute lockout on breach); separate per-UID-per-second limits by product/VIP tier (default 10/s futures & options, 20/s spot; scales to 40–60/s at VIP tiers). VERIFIED (bybit-exchange.github.io/docs/v5).
- **OKX:** Public/IP endpoints ~20 req/2 sec; private/UID endpoints vary by call (order placement 60 req/2 sec standard); sub-account order cap of 1,000 orders/2 sec (expandable to 10,000/2 sec only at VIP8+ with high fill ratio). Global ceiling ~250 req/sec. Error 50011/50061 on breach; no ban is explicitly documented — client must implement backoff. VERIFIED (okx.com/docs-v5).
- **Coinbase (Advanced Trade/Exchange):** Public REST 10 req/sec/IP (burst 15); private 15 req/sec/profile (burst 30); WebSocket 8 connections/sec/IP. Coinbase App API: 10,000 req/hour/key. 429 on breach; docs note limits "may tighten dynamically during high-volume periods" — i.e., budget can shrink exactly when volatility spikes. VERIFIED (docs.cdp.coinbase.com).
- **Kraken:** Counter-based system that decays over time; tier-dependent (Starter/Intermediate/Pro), separate counters for REST calls and for order placement/cancellation ("trading rate limit," heavily penalizes cancel-heavy strategies). `EAPI:Rate limit exceeded` / `EService:Throttled` on breach; no stated auto-ban, but sustained violation extends the lockout indefinitely. VERIFIED (docs.kraken.com).

**Implication for a multi-frequency system:** all five exchanges rate-limit by IP and/or UID, and Kraken/OKX explicitly penalize cancel/amend order churn more than new orders — a naive multi-strategy bot bursting three frequency bands from one IP will hit Binance's IP-shared weight budget and Bybit's 600/5s IP cap before hitting any per-strategy logic, because those two limits are exchange-wide, not per-key.

## 3. Withdrawal freezes (beyond FTX)

- **OKEx, Oct 16 – Nov 26, 2020 (~5.5 weeks):** All withdrawals suspended exchange-wide because one of three multisig private-key holders (founder Star Xu) was detained by Chinese police for an unrelated investigation. Trading continued; withdrawals did not, for over a month. VERIFIED (CoinDesk, Bloomberg, Nasdaq, Oct–Nov 2020). This is the single clearest documented case of a top-10 exchange freezing withdrawals for weeks due to key-management/governance failure, not a hack or insolvency.
- **Binance, June 13, 2022 (~3.5 hours):** BTC withdrawals paused during the Celsius-driven crypto sell-off, officially due to a "stuck on-chain transaction"/hardware failure on wallet-consolidation nodes; Binance's own postmortem confirmed the cause. Coincided with panic as Celsius froze withdrawals the same week. VERIFIED (CNBC, Decrypt, Bitcoin.com, June 13–14 2022).
- **Bybit, Feb 21–22, 2025 (hack + bank run):** $1.5B stolen from a cold wallet (Lazarus Group, per Elliptic); triggered $5.5B in total outflows within ~24h — a genuine bank run. Safe{Wallet} itself froze certain smart-wallet functionality mid-crisis, temporarily blocking Bybit's own access to $3B in reserves needed to fund withdrawals. Bybit processed 350k withdrawal requests, 99.9% within 10 hours, backed by an emergency bridge loan — but for a window, withdrawal capacity was constrained by a *third-party* infrastructure freeze (Safe), not Bybit's choice. VERIFIED (CNBC, CoinDesk, Cointelegraph, Feb 21–22 2025).

## 4. Post-FTX counterparty risk management

**Proof of Reserves — credible but structurally incomplete.** Academic work (Broken Proofs of Solvency, IACR ePrint 2022/043) documents real implementation flaws (weak hashing, Maxwell-Todd summation-tree manipulation letting an exchange under-report liabilities) in production PoR systems used by named exchanges (BHEX/HBTC, Coinfloor). More fundamentally: PoR is a single-day snapshot of *assets*, built from the exchange's own internal ledger, and it does not verify off-chain liabilities, bank debts, or that funds weren't borrowed just for the snapshot ("window dressing"). Mazars — the auditor doing Binance's PoR attestations — paused all crypto attestation work in December 2022, and Binance subsequently said Big Four firms would not take on the engagement. VERIFIED (multiple sources: news.bitcoin.com Dec 2022, LegalClarity, Cobo, The Block). Bottom line: PoR proves "we hold coins that were counted," not "we are solvent." Treat every exchange PoR page as a necessary-but-far-from-sufficient signal.

**Exchange diversification and automated sweeps** are standard practiced controls at institutional desks post-FTX — splitting capital across venues caps single-counterparty loss, and scheduled sweeps to self-custody or a qualified custodian reduce standing exchange balance. Both are operationally simple to implement in an automated system (cron-based withdrawal jobs, per-exchange caps enforced at the position-sizing layer).

**Institutional custody at $100k–$1M is mostly not cost-effective.** Actual minimums: Coinbase Prime/Custody ~$500K minimum (2026 pricing page cites 50bps + $0–10K setup); BitGo ~$100K–$1M minimum depending on source, 0.25–0.40%/yr; Fireblocks/Copper ~$1M+ minimum, infrastructure-model pricing; Anchorage $10M minimum, 15–30bps + $3,000/month minimum fee — meaning a $100K account would pay a minimum ~3%/year just in custody fees before any AUC discount kicks in. VERIFIED (edouard.ai comparison, spark.money, SEC-filed Anchorage fee schedule, rfp.wiki). At $100K–$1M, qualified custodians are either unavailable (below minimum) or economically irrational (fee drag of several hundred bps/year). Self-custody (a hardware wallet or simple multisig) is the only realistic off-exchange option at this scale.

## 5. Practical recommendation for $100K–$1M across 5–7 exchanges

- **Per-exchange capital caps: yes, and simple.** Cap any single exchange at roughly 25–30% of total capital (implies 4+ venues minimum for full deployment) — proportionate to account size, cheap to implement, directly bounds the OKEx/Bybit/FTX-style tail event.
- **Off-exchange cold storage between sessions: only partially realistic.** Full sweep-to-cold-storage between every trading session conflicts directly with needing capital deployed — crypto trades 24/7 and there is no clean "session boundary." More realistic: keep a **working balance on each exchange sized to near-term strategy needs**, and auto-sweep the *excess above a rolling threshold* (e.g., anything above 110% of trailing 7-day peak margin usage) to self-custody on a schedule (daily/weekly), not continuously. This is what "diversification + scheduled sweeps" means in practice at this account size — not zero-balance-at-rest, which is unworkable for an active strategy.
- **Skip institutional custody entirely at this size** — the fee drag (multiple hundred bps/year, or outright below-minimum) is not justified until the account is meaningfully larger (roughly $5M+ where Anchorage/BitGo economics start making sense). Use a well-audited multisig (Gnosis Safe or similar — note Safe itself was implicated in the Bybit incident, so don't treat it as risk-free) or a hardware-wallet-backed setup for the off-exchange balance instead.
- **Rate-limit headroom, not just compliance:** build API clients to stay well under 50% of documented limits during normal operation, specifically because Binance's IP-shared weight budget and Bybit's IP-level 600/5s cap mean a burst from *one* strategy can lock out *all* strategies on that exchange — the ban is IP/UID-wide, not per-strategy.
- **Assume every exchange has a bad hour during your worst days.** October 10, 2025 shows even Binance — the most liquid, most "too big to fail"-feeling venue — froze up for roughly an hour during the exact conditions an autonomous system most needs to act (cascading liquidations, oracle/collateral depegs). Design explicit circuit breakers: if an exchange's API returns elevated error rates or price deviates >X% from a reference/oracle feed, halt new orders on that venue and fail safe (do not blindly retry into a degraded matching engine) rather than assuming "it'll come back."
