This visualization is the MacroHFT Regime Fracture Landscape: an interactive research simulation showing how a hypothetical multi-agent HFT system could respond as market regimes change.

The landscape covers 2000–2026 using rolling six-year windows. Its historical stress signal is derived from S&P market behavior, CPI, and interest-rate conditions. It does not use crypto order-book data, execute trades, or train a live AI.

Every point on the upper surface represents a simulated model configuration.

The X axis is hyper-agent arbitration temperature, which controls how concentrated or distributed the model’s sub-agent selection is.

The Y axis is macro-volatility sensitivity, which controls how strongly the system reacts to changing market stress.

The surface height and color represent a deterministic simulated HFT objective. Cool, lower basins represent more stable configurations. Bright ridges and fractures represent higher simulated shock response, agent conflict, slippage stress, drawdown risk, or execution churn.

Five glowing nodes represent specialist HFT sub-agents. Their relative weights change across the timeline. The central hyper-agent marker shows the current arbitration point, while the optimizer path tracks how the lowest-loss basin migrates from one regime window to the next.

Shock wavefronts and vertical fracture pillars highlight unstable regions of the parameter space. The lower sheet visualizes the corresponding regime-memory and tail-pressure field.

The interactive modes separate:

robust loss, shock-response penalty, sub-agent conflict, slippage stress, drawdown risk, and execution churn.

This is a visual model of how an HFT architecture could search for a stable parameter basin during market chaos. It is not proof of real alpha, a live trading system, or an actual trained AI.
