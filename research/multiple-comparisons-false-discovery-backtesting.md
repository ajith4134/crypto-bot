# The Multiple-Comparisons / False-Discovery Problem in Automated Trading-Strategy Search

**Provenance:** Researched by a Sonnet subagent, 2026-08-01, via WebSearch/WebFetch. Part of a 5-agent parallel sweep on autonomous strategy-discovery systems for a $100k-$1M crypto trading project. This is the single most important section of the sweep. Report-only, no files modified by the subagent. Citations verified against publisher/SSRN listings during the research session.

## 1. The core problem, stated precisely

If you backtest N strategies/configurations against the same historical data and select the highest in-sample Sharpe, the selected Sharpe is not an estimate of true expected performance — it is the **maximum of N noisy draws**, most of which is sampling variance, not skill. A population with zero true edge will, by chance, produce a highest observed Sharpe that grows with N.

**Intuition/math**: expected maximum of N ~iid Gaussian-like draws grows roughly on the order of √(2·ln N) in standardized units (classical extreme-value-theory result) — grows without bound as N→∞, though only slowly (logarithmically): enough trials always eventually produce an arbitrarily impressive in-sample Sharpe from noise alone. Separately, in a fixed-threshold framing: at α=0.05, testing 1,000 independent zero-skill strategies yields an expected ~50 "significant" false positives by chance (N·α); at 10,000 trials, ~500. Automated search compounds both effects — it doesn't just cross a fixed bar by chance, it actively selects the single luckiest noise draw (max-of-N, the more severe version).

**Foundational citations**:

- **Bailey, D.H. & López de Prado, M. (2014). "The Deflated Sharpe Ratio: Correcting for Selection Bias, Backtest Overfitting and Non-Normality." Journal of Portfolio Management, 40(5), 94–107.** Formalizes the DSR: probability an observed Sharpe is genuinely significant after controlling for (a) number of trials N, (b) variance of Sharpes across trials, (c) non-normality (skew/kurtosis) which further inflates naive Sharpe estimates. Companion: Bailey & López de Prado, "The Sharpe Ratio Efficient Frontier," Journal of Risk 15(2), Winter 2012/13 — gives **Minimum Backtest Length (MinBTL)**: MinBTL ≲ 2·ln(N) / (expected Sharpe)². Their illustrative example: with 5 years of data, trying more than ~45 independent configurations makes it near-certain you'll find one with in-sample annualized Sharpe 1.0 whose true OOS Sharpe is zero.
- **Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu (2014). "Pseudo-Mathematics and Financial Charlatanism: The Effects of Backtest Overfitting on Out-of-Sample Performance." Notices of the American Mathematical Society, 61(5), 458–471.** Names "backtest overfitting" plainly: "high simulated performance is easily achievable after backtesting a relatively small number of alternative strategy configurations... a large proportion of backtests published in academic journals may be misleading. The situation is not likely to be better among practitioners."
- **Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu, "The Probability of Backtest Overfitting." Journal of Computational Finance, 20(4), 39–69 (2017; SSRN preprint 2014).** Introduces **combinatorially symmetric cross-validation (CSCV)** to estimate **Probability of Backtest Overfitting (PBO)** — probability the in-sample-best configuration underperforms the median configuration OOS. Direct ancestor of CPCV.
- **White, H. (2000). "A Reality Check for Data Snooping." Econometrica, 68(5), 1097–1126.** Foundational general-econometrics treatment (predates the LdP program by 14 years): formalizes "data snooping," gives a bootstrap-based procedure (Reality Check) testing whether the *best* of many competing models has genuine predictive superiority over a benchmark.
- **Hansen, P.R. (2005). "A Test for Superior Predictive Ability." Journal of Business & Economic Statistics, 23(4), 365–380.** Successor to White's RC: SPA test is more powerful, less distorted by padding the candidate pool with obviously-bad strategies (a common RC flaw) — directly relevant since automated search generates enormous numbers of low-quality candidates alongside any genuinely promising ones.
- **Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016). "…and the Cross-Section of Expected Returns." Review of Financial Studies, 29(1), 5–68.** Applied to the equity "factor zoo": conventional t≈2.0 significance bar is invalid once accounting for cumulative multiple testing across the published literature since 1967; argue newly discovered factors need **t-stat > 3.0**. Explicit medical-research analogy: most claimed findings in financial economics are likely false without adjustment. Generalizes directly: an in-house automated search accumulating thousands of backtests should treat naive t≈2 as meaningless and calibrate the bar to actual trial count, exactly as DSR does.

## 2. Purged/Embargoed k-fold CV and Combinatorial Purged Cross-Validation (CPCV)

Primary source: **López de Prado, M. (2018). Advances in Financial Machine Learning. Wiley.** (Cross-validation chapter covers purging/embargo; backtesting chapters develop CPCV on the CSCV/PBO framework above.)

**Why standard k-fold CV leaks in financial time series**: assumes i.i.d. observations. False in finance because (1) labels are forward-looking and overlapping — a label computed from a future price window can overlap in time with a test-fold observation's window, leaking information; (2) features/residuals are serially correlated — rolling features and price-process autocorrelation mean adjacent-in-time observations carry redundant information even without explicit label overlap. Net effect: naive k-fold CV produces optimistic OOS scores because "out-of-sample" isn't actually independent of "in-sample."

**Purging**: remove training observations whose label-evaluation window overlaps in time with any test-fold observation's window.

**Embargo**: exclude a further short window of training data immediately following a test fold, since autocorrelation can still leak backward information forward.

**What CPCV adds**: rather than one split or a single k-fold rotation, CPCV partitions data into N groups and enumerates combinatorial train/test group assignments of fixed test-group size, purging/embargoing each, producing a full **empirical distribution of OOS Sharpe ratios across many distinct backtest paths** — rather than trusting a single lucky/unlucky historical path. Feeds a variance estimate into DSR and a PBO estimate.

## 3. Practical structural controls for an automated discovery system

- **DSR as the live scoring function** (not a bolt-on post-check): score every candidate by DSR, tracking N (total trials including failed/discarded ones — easy to under-count), variance across trials' Sharpes, and non-normality of the candidate's own returns.
- **Minimum Track Record Length (MinTRL)**: Bailey & López de Prado's own term (Journal of Risk 15(2), 2012/13) — minimum return observations needed before an observed Sharpe can be statistically distinguished from a benchmark at a chosen confidence level. Actionable: gates how long a strategy must run (paper or live) before you're entitled to trust its Sharpe, which should gate any capital-increase decision.
- **Bonferroni correction**: divide α by N. Simple but nearly useless at automated-system scale — at N=10,000, α/N=0.000005 suppresses essentially everything including true positives.
- **Benjamini-Hochberg (BH/BHY) false discovery rate control**: controls expected proportion of false positives among accepted discoveries, far more practical at scale. Not generic — Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016) explicitly apply Bonferroni/Holm/BHY corrections alongside the t>3.0 heuristic. Should be applied to a search system's "promoted" list, not just a single fixed t-stat cutoff.
- **A true untouched final holdout set**: a slice of history (or later time period, or paper-trading itself) that the search process — including meta-parameters and human tuning — never sees until one candidate is already selected, looked at exactly once. Practical embodiment of what White's RC / Hansen's SPA statistically substitute for.
- **CPCV as the standard evaluation harness** for every candidate — Sharpe distribution not point estimate, PBO computed per CSCV — reject high-PBO candidates regardless of best-path Sharpe.
- **Walk-forward analysis**: sequential, strictly-forward re-optimization/testing, both a robustness check and closer simulation of live redeployment/re-tuning.
- **Staged OOS paper-trading before capital allocation**: an additional holdout layer, since backtests — however well cross-validated — still inherit look-ahead bias in feature engineering, survivorship bias in instrument universe, and execution-cost mis-modeling no amount of CV fully removes. Only real-time forward performance is immune to those specific biases (though it introduces its own noise, which MinTRL logic should be applied to before trusting).

## 4. The critical verdict

**No — testing thousands of strategy variants per day/week without these controls engineered into the search loop itself (not as a final gate on the winner) is not statistically sound, and will produce backtests that look excellent and lose money live.**

- The arithmetic compounds with automation: naive α=0.05, 1,000 no-skill trials → ~50 apparent false positives by chance; an "AlphaEvolve for trading"-style system running thousands of mutated variants/day produces that many trials in hours, hundreds of thousands over weeks. Worse, evolutionary/mutation search selects the best of N each generation — the more severe max-of-N problem — and the effective N compounds across generations if fitness isn't corrected every generation. Computing DSR only once, on the final winner, has already thrown away the information needed to correct for true cumulative N.
- **Crypto-specific aggravation**: most liquid pairs have only a few years of high-quality historical data (vs. equities' decades); 24/7 trading and frequent regime shifts increase effective noise; many "independent" variants actually test correlated signals across correlated assets (altcoins co-moving with BTC/ETH), reducing effective independent N somewhat — but this does not remotely offset the raw combinatorial explosion of testing thousands of variants; if anything it makes CPCV-style path-based robustness checking more necessary, since a single historical path is an even less reliable sample of "what could have happened" in a young, regime-shifting market.
- **The fix is structural, not cosmetic**: deflating the final selected strategy's Sharpe after the fact is necessary but insufficient if the search process itself never tracked N, never purged/embargoed CV folds, and never distinguished genuine forward-only walk-forward evaluation from repeated reuse of the same historical window across mutation generations. DSR/PBO/CPCV/MinTRL/BH-FDR/true-holdout/staged-paper-trading need to be load-bearing components of the search-and-promotion pipeline, gating what gets promoted to the next generation — not just annotating what got shipped.
