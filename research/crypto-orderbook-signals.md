## 1. Order-Book / Order-Flow Imbalance (OBI/OFI)

**Foundational work**: Cont, Kukanov & Stoikov, "The Price Impact of Order Book Events," *Journal of Financial Econometrics* 12(1), 2014 (SSRN posted 2010) — 50 NYSE stocks, showed OFI (net change in bid/ask queue size from limit orders, market orders, and cancellations) linearly explains mid-price changes with average R²≈65%, slope inversely proportional to depth. **VERIFIED** as the canonical equity result.

**Crypto validation — genuine and multiple**: Silantyev, "Order flow analysis of cryptocurrency markets," *Digital Finance* 1(1), 2019 (BitMEX XBTUSD) directly replicates the Cont-Kukanov-Stoikov framework and finds a caveat specific to crypto: *trade* flow imbalance explains contemporaneous price changes better than aggregate OFI, and the paper explicitly attributes divergence from equities to "lack of depth and low update arrival rates" in crypto books. **VERIFIED**. Bieganowski & Ślepaczuk, "Explainable Patterns in Cryptocurrency Microstructure," arXiv 2602.00776 (2026), find OFI has a stable, monotone SHAP effect on 1-second BTC/LTC/ETC/ENJ/ROSE Binance Futures returns, and — notably — that OFI's predictive strength *increases* with tick size, consistent with the microprice mechanism. **VERIFIED**. A University of Toronto working paper, "Fragmentation, Price Formation, and Cross-Impact in Bitcoin Markets," goes further and argues the *classical* top-of-book OBI formula is actively unsuitable in crypto: during volatility breakouts, top-of-book size collapses to near-zero on one side, making the ratio wildly noisy in a way rarely seen in deeper equity books; they propose a depth-robust replacement. **VERIFIED**, and this is the most direct rebuttal of naive equity-style OBI transferring to crypto.

**The bot/cancellation problem is empirically documented, not speculative**: Nejat & Breton (2024, HEC Montréal working paper) and a 2025 arXiv paper on LOB "spoofability" (2504.15908) both use real crypto order-book data (Bitcoin, and crypto CEX Level-3 data respectively) to show OBI can be manipulated — the second study estimates 31% of large orders in their December 2024 sample could profitably spoof the imbalance signal, and that sophisticated spoofers place orders *away* from best price specifically to avoid detection while still moving the imbalance. **VERIFIED**: this directly substantiates the concern that crypto OBI is contaminated by strategic cancellation in a way the original 2014 equity study (regulated, less bot-dominated NYSE) didn't need to model. A non-peer-reviewed GitHub study (btc-orderbook-research, 38 days of Binance BTC/USDT data) reports OBI autocorrelation decaying to noise floor in ~26 seconds and Spearman correlation with forward returns dropping from ~0.20 (10s) to ~0.075 (60s) — **UNVERIFIED** as a rigorous result (no peer review, single exchange/period) but directionally consistent with the peer-reviewed literature's message that crypto OBI's edge is short-lived and level-1-only versions are fragile.

**Verdict**: OBI/OFI is the signal with the most genuine crypto-specific empirical support — but only in multi-level or robustified form, not the naive best-bid/best-ask ratio, which the Toronto paper and the spoofing literature both show breaks down.

## 2. Microprice

**Foundational work**: Stoikov, "The Micro-Price: A High-Frequency Estimator of Future Prices," *Quantitative Finance* 18(12), 2018 (working paper 2017) — equities BAC and CVX, shown to beat mid-price and weighted mid-price as a short-term fair-value estimator.

**Crypto validation**: "Mind the Gaps: Short-Term Crypto Price Prediction" (SSRN 4351947) is a genuine crypto-specific test using Bitstamp BTC LOB data; it constructs three microprice-family estimators and finds the Volume-Adjusted Mid-Price (VAMP) variant outperforms both trade- and quote-imbalance-adjusted microprice for short-term direction and large moves. **VERIFIED**, though note this paper's headline finding is that a *variant* beats Stoikov's original formulation, not that the original wins outright. Beyond this, the crypto "evidence" is thin: replications exist only as informal GitHub repos (ETHBTC/TRXBTC on Binance, BitMEX quote calibration) with no statistical significance testing or out-of-sample rigor — these are **UNVERIFIED**, illustrative-only, and should not be treated as validation.

**Crypto-specific frictions, reasoned but not directly tested in a dedicated paper**: Stoikov's model discretizes the imbalance/spread state space calibrated to a fixed tick size; the Toronto fragmentation paper independently documents that crypto venues use materially different tick sizes (e.g., $1 vs $0.1 on different BTC venues at the time studied) and that this complicates any imbalance-weighting scheme across venues. **VERIFIED** that tick-size heterogeneity is real and documented, but **UNVERIFIED** that it specifically breaks microprice calibration — I could not find a paper that isolates this effect for microprice itself. Similarly, the argument that higher crypto quote-cancellation rates degrade the imbalance term microprice depends on is **UNVERIFIED** as a dedicated study — it follows by construction from the OBI-manipulability evidence above (microprice is literally an OBI-weighted adjustment to mid-price), but no one has run "does spoofing degrade microprice specifically" as its own test.

**Verdict**: real but sparse crypto-specific validation, one solid SSRN paper, everything else is either equity-transfer reasoning or unreviewed replication code.

## 3. Kyle's Lambda

**Foundational work**: Kyle, "Continuous Auctions and Insider Trading," *Econometrica* 53(6), 1985.

**Crypto estimates exist, but the news is that it's a weak signal, not that it's absent**: Easley, López de Prado, O'Hara & co-authors (the "Microstructure and Market Dynamics in Crypto Markets" paper, Cornell/SSRN, forthcoming *Journal of Financial Markets/ScienceDirect* 2026) estimate Kyle's lambda for BTC, ETH, XRP, SOL, ADA on Binance and explicitly report: "the own Kyle measure typically has a low, nearly-zero MDA score" in random-forest feature-importance tests predicting volatility/autocorrelation dynamics — i.e., it's estimable but not independently predictive once Roll measure and VPIN are included. **VERIFIED**. A separate WFE (World Federation of Exchanges) working paper on DLT settlement latency estimates Kyle's lambda for BTC/ETH/LTC across Coinbase, FTX, and Kraken and finds it responds sensibly to a crypto-specific driver (blockchain settlement/confirmation latency) that has no equity analogue — a genuinely crypto-native validation that the quantity is estimable and economically meaningful as a liquidity descriptor. **VERIFIED**. The 2026 Frontiers-in-Blockchain "Microstructure alpha" paper similarly finds Kyle's lambda is *not* individually significant (p<0.05) in a hierarchical return-prediction model across six crypto assets, contributing only "at the margin" to stability selection. **VERIFIED**.

**Verdict**: Kyle's lambda is stably estimable in 24/7 crypto markets (multiple independent papers do it), but the weight of crypto-specific evidence says it's a liquidity/impact *descriptor*, not a standalone predictive alpha signal — every study that tests it against other features finds it redundant or near-zero once OFI, Roll measure, or VPIN are present.

## 4. VPIN

**Foundational work**: Easley, López de Prado & O'Hara, "Flow Toxicity and Liquidity in a High-Frequency World," *Review of Financial Studies* 25(5), 2012.

**The controversy is real and unresolved, even in equities**: Andersen & Bondarenko, "VPIN and the Flash Crash," *Journal of Financial Markets* 17, 2014, and their follow-up "Reflecting on the VPIN Dispute," *Journal of Financial Markets* 17, 2014, using S&P 500 futures tick data, argue VPIN is *by construction* correlated with trading volume and volatility innovations, has **no incremental predictive power** for volatility once volume/volatility are controlled for, and — most damagingly — did not reach an all-time high *before* the 2010 flash crash but *after* it. ELO published a rejoinder disputing this ("VPIN and the Flash Crash: A rejoinder," *Journal of Financial Markets*) calling AB's critique a misapplication of a methodology ELO themselves don't advocate. **VERIFIED**: this is a live, unresolved academic dispute between the metric's inventors and a serious empirical challenge, not a settled question — treat "VPIN works" as contested even on its home turf.

**Crypto-specific studies exist and lean positive, but come with a caveat**: Kitvanitphasu, Kyaw, Likitapiwat & Treepongkaruna, "Bitcoin wild moves: Evidence from order flow toxicity and price jumps," *Research in International Business and Finance*, 2025, use VAR modeling on high-frequency Bitcoin data and find VPIN significantly predicts future price jumps, robust across multiple jump-test specifications. **VERIFIED**, peer-reviewed, genuinely crypto-specific. The Easley/O'Hara Cornell 2026 paper finds VPIN is the second-most-important predictor (by MDA) of crypto volatility/autocorrelation dynamics across five cryptocurrencies, with strong BTC↔ETH cross-market effects, and reports VPIN levels are notably *higher* in crypto than in equities/futures. **VERIFIED**. The caveat: neither crypto paper engages with the Andersen-Bondarenko "mechanical correlation with volume/volatility" critique — and one of the crypto papers is co-authored by O'Hara herself, the metric's co-inventor, raising the same "is this independent validation or the same camp re-confirming its own tool" concern that exists in the equity literature. I found **no crypto-specific equivalent of an Andersen-Bondarenko rebuttal** — this is **UNVERIFIED as a gap**: it's plausible the same mechanical-correlation critique applies to crypto VPIN (crypto volume/volatility are at least as persistent and autocorrelated as equity futures), but no one has run that test yet.

**Verdict**: VPIN has real crypto-specific supportive evidence, but it inherits an unresolved foundational controversy that the crypto literature has not yet engaged with. Calling it "discredited" would overstate the equity-side critique's reach; calling it "settled science" ignores that critique entirely.

## 5. Ranking for an Autonomous Crypto Trading System

1. **OFI / order-flow imbalance (multi-level, robustified version)** — strongest, most direct crypto-specific evidence (Silantyev 2019, Bieganowski & Ślepaczuk 2026, Toronto fragmentation paper), *provided* you use a depth-weighted or robust variant, not naive best-bid/ask OBI. Must be paired with spoofing-awareness (Nejat & Breton 2024; arXiv 2504.15908) since crypto order books are demonstrably gameable at the level-1 OBI relies on, and the signal's edge decays in tens of seconds, not minutes.
2. **Microprice (VAMP-style adjustment)** — one solid crypto-specific SSRN validation, but thin overall; treat the specific tick-size and cancellation-rate concerns as reasoned-but-untested rather than proven, and prefer volume-adjusted variants over Stoikov's original discretized formulation given crypto's tick-size heterogeneity across venues.
3. **Kyle's lambda** — reliably *estimable* in crypto (several independent papers construct it, and it responds sensibly to crypto-native drivers like settlement latency), but the crypto literature consistently finds it economically weak/redundant as a standalone predictor once OFI, Roll measure, or VPIN are included. Useful as a liquidity-regime descriptor for execution/sizing, not as an alpha signal.
4. **VPIN** — the most contested of the four. Genuine crypto-specific peer-reviewed support exists, but it sits on top of an unresolved and serious equity-market critique (Andersen & Bondarenko) that the crypto literature hasn't yet addressed. This is the signal most at risk of being **cargo-culted**: it's popular partly because Easley/López de Prado/O'Hara are prestigious and prolific, and partly because "flow toxicity" is an intuitively appealing story — not because the mechanical-correlation objection has been resolved. Use with explicit awareness you're relying on a metric its own inventors are still defending against a credible attack.

**Overall honesty check**: none of these four signals has the kind of decisive, adversarially-tested crypto validation that would let you deploy it blind. OFI is the closest to defensible. Everything past that trades off "more studies exist" against "the studies that exist find it weak or contested."
